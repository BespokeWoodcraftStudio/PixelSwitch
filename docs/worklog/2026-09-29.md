## 07:40 PDT · WL-2026-09-29-001 · Logged for a later session: a 503 from the usage server shows a raw Swift error ('UsageError error 0')

**Type:** research · **Status:** done · **Tags:** defect,usage,error-message,handover

### What
Diagnosed the card error the founder saw on ahamade@gmail.com: "Could not fetch usage: The operation couldn't be completed. (PixelSwitch.ClaudeService.UsageError error 0.)". Recorded it as the next fix to build in `docs/SESSION-HANDOVER.md`, under "Next fix to build", with the evidence, the root cause and a proposed fix. No code was changed; the founder said "don't fix, just answer", then asked for it to be logged for another session.

### Why
The founder couldn't tell what was wrong or what to do. What he wants next time, in his words: "the person understands what they need to do just by a simple double-click or something like that, or by clicking the refresh."

### How
- Read `~/Library/Logs/PixelSwitch-app.log`. The app's FileLog writes there, not to the unified log, so `log show` finds nothing.
- Found 7 × `[getUsageLimits] HTTP 503` between 14:17 and 14:36 UTC on 2026-09-29, across four accounts. The gmail account's saved login was healthy the whole time.
- Traced the text on the card: `ClaudeService.getUsageLimits` turns any status other than 401, 403 or 429 into `UsageError.network("HTTP 503")`. `UsageError` isn't a `LocalizedError`, so Swift prints its generic message. The catch-all at `AppState.swift:1549-1553` shows that text and clears the last reading.

### Outcome
The cause is an outage on Anthropic's side; it is transient and not an account problem. The fix is specified in the handover and has not been built: a plain message per failure kind (5xx, offline, unexpected), keep the last reading the way a 429 does, a Retry control on the card itself, and a status.claude.com link for a 5xx. Tests first; ship as 1.7.

### Lessons / gotchas
- "UsageError error N" is **not** an HTTP code. For a Swift enum bridged to NSError, the cases with a payload are numbered first, so `error 0` = `.network` here (`expired`, which has no payload, is numbered last).
- The app's own log is `~/Library/Logs/PixelSwitch-app.log`. Grep `getUsageLimits] HTTP` there to tally failures by status. On 2026-09-29 it held 401 ×24, 403 ×148, 429 ×2, 503 ×7.
- Losing the reading on a 5xx is worse than a stale number: it's the same trade-off already decided for 429.

- [ ] Build the fix described in SESSION-HANDOVER.md, "Next fix to build" (release 1.7, plus a hand-check card).

## 10:02 PDT · WL-2026-09-29-003 · Question page: should auto-switch always move at the founder's number (drop the 10-point gap)?

**Type:** decision · **Status:** wip · **Tags:** auto-switch,founder-question

### What
Answered the founder's question "if I set my switch number to 98% or 99%, it should switch on that number, shouldn't it?", and put the decision it raises on a question page: `docs/decisions/pixelswitch-switch-point-2026-09-29.html`. The page has one card, three options (the recommended one first), a comment box, and a Copy button.

### Why
The answer is only "yes" when a roomy account exists. The threshold is when auto-switch starts looking, from `AutoSwitchSettings.leaveAtThreshold`, checked every 300 s. A target must be at `ceiling(threshold, room: 10)` or lower, which is 88% at 98%. On the Beeco week every account was at 92–99%, so the switch only happens at 100% (the 1.7 `exhausted` rule). Whether to drop the gap is his call, and his standing rule says questions go on a page.

### How
- `scripts/decisions/gen-switch-point.py` builds the page from the founder template, changing only PAGE.
- `scripts/decisions/render-decision-page.cjs` is a generic copy of the hand-check renderer: page path, out-dir and card id as arguments. It renders at 1920×1080 in light and dark, and tests Copy.

### Outcome
- Rendered in both themes with no errors and no horizontal scroll.
- The Copy block came back with the answer and the comment.
- Listed in SESSION-HANDOVER.md under "Waiting on the founder", item 2.

### Decisions & trade-offs
- Recommended: drop the gap. Flip-flopping is impossible anyway: an account is left only at or over its threshold and a target must be under its own, and there is a 5-minute cooldown.
- The cost is more switches on a full week, each to an account with little room.
- Alternatives on the page: a 3-point gap, or keep 1.7.
